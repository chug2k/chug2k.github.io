The locksmith slid the spare across the counter. Ada felt uneasy about giving her brother a key. She dropped it into the envelope marked **BEN**.

You know how Ada feels because I told you. Give her one more thing to do with the key and see what you think of her.

<!--more-->

> The locksmith slid the spare across the counter. Ada threaded it onto her own key ring. The clerk was already cutting a new blank when she worked her thumbnail between the coils again. She dropped the spare into the envelope marked **BEN**.

Where did you start to suspect she might keep it? For me it’s **threaded**. That takes long enough to feel like a choice. Then there’s the thumbnail. She has to undo a small piece of work to give Ben what she came to buy him.

A reader might decide Ada put the key on her ring out of habit. I like that reading too. If the rest of the story depends on Ada distrusting Ben, I’d give the reader a different clue. She could copy the number stamped on the spare onto her receipt before she seals the envelope. That would give Ben something interesting to find later.

## Try it with a scene

The revision desk opens at a radio repair counter. A teenager has fixed her first radio. She wants the customer to hear it play before he takes it home. You can also bring a paragraph of your own. [Open the revision desk.](/revision-desk/)

1. Keep the last thing that happens. The customer still leaves with the radio, just as Ben still gets his envelope.
2. Find the instant before that happens. Give the character a small action with something already in reach. Try writing two sentences.
3. Write down what a stranger might infer. Select the exact words that gave the stranger that idea.

If you can quote the clue, try cutting the sentence that explains it. Read the passage aloud once. Sometimes the explanation was carrying something the gesture hasn’t earned yet. You can put it back.
